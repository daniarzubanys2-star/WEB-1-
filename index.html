Build the computer you actually need

MyPC Lab is my small workshop notebook about assembling desktop computers from separate parts. Here I collect what I learned while planning, buying and putting together my own machine.

What goes inside a PC

A desktop computer is not magic. It is seven or eight parts that talk to each other through the motherboard. Once you know what each part does, choosing them stops being scary.

  • CPU — the processor, it does the calculations.
  • GPU — the video card, it draws games and video.
  • RAM — fast temporary memory for open programs.
  • SSD — permanent storage for the system and files.
  • PSU — the power supply that feeds everything.

How I build a machine

I always follow the same order. It keeps cables tidy and saves me from taking the whole thing apart again because I forgot one screw.

  1. Decide the budget and what the PC is for: study, games or video.
  2. Pick the CPU first, then a motherboard with the same socket.
  3. Add RAM, SSD and a video card that the power supply can feed.
  4. Mount the motherboard in the case, then the PSU and the drives.
  5. Connect the front panel cables and close the side panel last.
  6. Start the PC, enter BIOS and check that all parts are detected.
